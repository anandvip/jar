## Core philosophy emphasizes habit over precision

The JAR system's enduring principle states: **"The habit of managing your money is much more important than the amount."** Eker teaches that percentages serve as guidelines rather than rigid rules, encouraging users to start with any amount—even $1—and adjust based on circumstances. [harveker](https://www.harveker.com/blog/6-step-money-managing-system/)

For those whose necessities exceed 55%, the guidance recommends starting wherever possible ($100 monthly, for instance) and gradually optimizing. The system accommodates debt by allocating payments from the LTSS jar while maintaining the habit across all categories. [harveker](https://www.harveker.com/blog/6-step-money-managing-system/) This prevents the common mistake of delaying financial management until debt disappears.

Real estate professionals and entrepreneurs report using a **dual-layer approach**: first separating business income into taxes (25%) and salary (25%), then applying the six-jar system to the salary portion. [T. Harv Eker Blog](https://www.harveker.com/blog/6-step-money-managing-system/)

Parents have adapted the framework for children as young as six, teaching financial literacy through scaled-down versions appropriate for allowances and gift money. [paulathedoctormom](https://www.paulathedoctormom.com/post/teaching-wealth-management-to-kids-the-6-jar-technique) [T. Harv Eker Blog](https://www.harveker.com/blog/6-jars-money-management-system-for-kids/)

The flexibility principle extends to credit cards—users can employ them if paying monthly balances, categorizing each charge by jar when statements arrive and transferring funds from respective accounts. [harveker](https://www.harveker.com/blog/6-step-money-managing-system/) This maintains system integrity while accommodating modern payment methods.
